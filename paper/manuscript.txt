Abstract—Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves
factual grounding for large language models, but standard
pipelines optimize primarily for lexical/semantic relevance and
ignore user affect. This mismatch can yield answers that are
technically supported yet emotionally misaligned. We present
Emotion-Aware RAG (EA-RAG), a modular framework that
injects emotion estimation into (i) query rewriting, (ii) retrieval
reranking, and (iii) tone-conditioned decoding policies. We instantiate EA-RAG with a transformer-based emotion classifier,
dense retrieval over a structured knowledge base constructed
from Reddit comments annotated with coarse affect clusters
(distress/neutral/positive), and deterministic generation for reproducibility. On a curated evaluation harness with seeded runs, EARAG shows stable retrieval behavior across random seeds while
improving simple emotion-alignment proxies on synthetic-style
prompts relative to heuristic baselines, and exposes clear ablation
knobs for future scaling to larger benchmarks and LLM judges.
Implementation is configuration-driven for reproducibility.
Index Terms—Retrieval-augmented generation, emotion recognition, conversational AI, reranking, controllable generation.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Large Language Models (LLMs) underpin modern assistants, yet hallucinations remain a persistent failure mode
under open-ended queries. Retrieval-Augmented Generation
mitigates this by conditioning answers on externally retrieved
passages [1]. Parallel progress in affective computing enables
reliable estimation of categorical affect in short text using
transformer classifiers trained on crowdsourced annotations
[2].
However, real users communicate with intertwined informational and emotional intent. Factually grounded yet emotionally insensitive responses can fail user-centered objectives
in education support, workplace assistance, and sensitive domains. Existing RAG systems rarely condition retrieval or
decoding on inferred affect, yielding a systemic gap between
affective HCI goals and grounding-centric engineering.
Contribution. We propose EA-RAG, which:
1) integrates emotion estimation into retrieval and generation with minimal architectural disruption;

2) introduces an interpretable reranking objective combining semantic similarity, emotional alignment, and a
distress-oriented retrieval prior;
3) provides reproducible benchmarking scripts, seeded
evaluation, qualitative inspection interfaces, and an open
implementation path toward stronger LLM judging (e.g.,
RAG evaluation suites [6]).

C. Evaluation Harness

We encode queries and passages with normalized dense
embeddings (default: BGE-style encoder) and retrieve top-k
candidates {di }ki=1 by inner product similarity.

Because fully manual annotation is expensive under student
timelines, we provide (i) small curated queries, (ii) autoconstructed grounding IDs for smoke testing, (iii) multi-seeded
metric aggregation scripts, (iv) an optional dashboard for
exploratory inspection.5

VI. D ISCUSSION
Pros. Modular affect signals slot cleanly into RAG
pipelines, enabling iterative engineering under compute limits.
Tone policies provide interpretable knobs for HCI evaluation.
Cons & Risks.
• Label circularity: auto-mined grounding sets can inflate
or distort rankings if not audited.
• Brief evaluation scale: reviewer expectations expect
thousands of annotated pairs for top venues.
• Bias & misuse: coarse affect labels flatten cultural emotional expression nuances.
VII. E THICAL C ONSIDERATIONS
Psychological-support scenarios require robust auditing, escalation to professionals, moderation, transparency, consent,
dataset governance, and harm modeling. Researchers must
refrain from implying clinical efficacy without IRB-reviewed
studies.
VIII. C ONCLUSION & F UTURE W ORK
We presented EA-RAG, combining affective inference with
retargeted retrieval and tonal generation. Immediate future
work integrates EmpatheticDialogues-compatible loaders, oracle annotations, multilingual affect taxonomies [5], and Ragasgrade metrics [6].
